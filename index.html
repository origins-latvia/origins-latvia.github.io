Origins: There and Back Again

Preface

This all began as an attempt to translate Dad’s diaries for any of his descendants who at some stage might be interested in them. It developed into the story of how Helēna Mackevica and Jānis Avens came to start a new life in Australia. As such their story forms part of the larger one of those displaced persons who migrated to Australia in the aftermath of WWII.

Whilst Dad’s travels are comparatively well documented, Mum’s are not, even to the extent that the file related to the voyage of the Protea, which brought her here, is no longer extant in the National Archives of Australia (NAA). For this reason her part in the narrative is unfortunately minor.

In the diary transcriptions which follow I try to represent what Dad actually wrote, whether or not I believe the spelling or details to be correct - spelling mistakes in the English translation or commentaries / annotations are mine alone.

Prologue

Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact

It started on August 23, 1939 with the Treaty of Non-Aggression between Germany and the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics whereby the 20th century’s greatest monsters, Hitler and Stalin divided up Europe between them and effectively began WWII.

Soviet occupation of Latvia in 1940-41

“June 17, 1940 - Soviet troops invade Latvia and occupy bridges, post/telephone, telegraph, and broadcasting offices .... The Soviet authorities, having gained control over Latvia, immediately imposed a regime of terror. Hundreds .... were arrested, including many leaders of the Republic of Latvia. Tribunals were set up to punish “traitors to the people.”.... The June deportation took place on June 13 and June 14, 1941, estimated at 15,600 men, women, and children, and including 20% of Latvia’s last legal government. Approximately 35,000 total (1.8% of Latvia’s population) were deported during the first Soviet occupation.” Source: Wikipedia

German occupation of Latvia in 1941-45

On 22 June, 1941 Hitler betrayed his co-conspirator Stalin and launched Operation Barbarossa, the invasion of Russia by Germany. Given the Russian reign of terror, the Germans were at first greeted as liberators by most Latvians. But they were about to be deceived.

Conscription and Consequence

“The punishment for draft evasion: “At a time when each true Latvian man who can carry a weapon is standing guard over his fatherland, three strong young men have chosen a different route... The Special War Tribunal of the General Inspectorate of the Latvian SS Volunteer Legion heard evidence against Edgars Vilciņš, Osvalds Streļķis and Jānis Vilciņš... The Special War Tribunal sentenced 26 year old Edgars Vilciņš and 23 year old Osvalds Streļķis to 15 years of hard labour...” Tēvija (Fatherland), 20 September 1944. Source: Latvian Ministry of Foreign Affairs (Retrieved 2010)
